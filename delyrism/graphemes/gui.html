Graphemes

Every stroke of a symbol, and every stretch of a movement, is cut into fragments and each fragment is written as a weighted sum of six motion shapes from one orthonormal wavelet basis. The shapes are scale-free, since every fragment is normalised to the same length, so a small wiggle reads as small S-bends and bows. Each shape can point in any of eight directions.

basis sym6 wavelet packetslearned on 19,280 drawings6 shapes × 8 directions + 3 lifts

Draw one

Draw with a mouse, a pen or a finger. Each stroke is cut where the six graphemes fit best. A dynamic program chooses the cut points that let the six rebuild every fragment most closely, with a small cost per cut, so a fine wiggle splits into its bends and a clean arc stays whole. The coloured line is what the six rebuild, coloured by the grapheme that bends each fragment most. The weights update as you draw.

The graphemes

Each card draws a straight stroke with one grapheme added across it, so you see the bend it makes. The bar strip is its velocity over the stroke, and the rosette shows it in its eight directions.

Mix them

A fragment is the straight grapheme plus any amount of the other five. Along the stroke a grapheme changes the pace, which you see in the moving pen. Across it, it bends the path. Pick a preset to see how familiar shapes decompose.

Your drawings, in graphemes

Each drawing is cut the same way, at pen lifts and wherever the six graphemes fit best, and every fragment is rebuilt from the six. Colour marks the grapheme that bends a fragment most, measured across the stroke, and fragments in ink colour are close to straight. Grey dots are your original line. Under each drawing, the ink bar measures the colours by length of line, and the energy bar is the grapheme distribution itself. The straight grapheme carries every fragment's forward motion, so it holds most of the energy even where the line is coloured.

Lifts

Three more graphemes carry the third axis, the pen leaving the drawing plane between strokes and coming back. Each plot is a side view, with height over the length of one lift.

The basis is the Coifman–Wickerhauser joint best basis of Symlet-6 wavelet packets over 60,000 stroke fragments from Omniglot's 30 training alphabets. Its atoms are orthonormal, so a fragment's energy splits exactly across the graphemes, and that split is the grapheme distribution used to weight symbols. Fragments are time-normalised to 32 steps and length-normalised before the transform.
